## HS400 on the 8MP EVK and the MEK

- Why: both SoCs' uSDHC have HS400 (Linux 6.18 sets `ESDHC_FLAG_HS400 | ESDHC_FLAG_HS400_ES`
  for `fsl,imx8mm-usdhc` and `fsl,imx8qxp-usdhc`; 8MP RM 10.3.6: MIX_CTRL bits 26/27,
  STROBE_DLL_CTRL/STATUS at 0x70/0x74), both boards wire the data strobe, and `mmc_emmc.c`
  had both HS400 sequences with no HAL that ran them (F-116, the audit's dead code).
- The cards, from Linux on the bench: the 8MP EVK's SanDisk DG4032 (EXT_CSD_REV 8, e.MMC
  5.1), which Linux runs in HS400 with Enhanced Strobe; the MEK's Micron R1J57L (REV 7, e.MMC
  5.0, DEVICE_TYPE 0x57, STROBE_SUPPORT 0), which upstream Linux runs in DDR52 only because
  its DTS has no 100/200 MHz pin states (`NO_1_8_V`).
- The driver: `imx_usdhc_config.hs400` (the SoC has it and the strobe is wired) gives the core
  `hs400` and `hs400_es`; the 8 bit DDR bus above 52 MHz is HS400: HS400_MODE (and
  EN_HS400_MODE with `MMC_BUS_WIDTH_8BIT_DDR_STROBE`) before the clock, the strobe DLL after
  it, as Linux's `esdhc_set_uhs_signaling()` and `esdhc_set_strobe_dll()` (target 7, update
  interval 4; no lock is an error here, a warning in Linux). `fixed_tap`: no tuning for one
  configuration. The core logs EXT_CSD_REV, DEVICE_TYPE and STROBE_SUPPORT (debug).
- The clock: in DDR the uSDHC divides by 2 at least (RM, SYS_CTRL.SDCLKFS), so HS400's 200 MHz
  needs 400 MHz in. The 8MP's USDHC3 root goes to 400 MHz (PLL1/2 undivided). The MEK's SDHC_0
  from PLL1 gives 333 MHz for 400 (HS200 then runs at 166 MHz); from PLL0 396 MHz.
- The boards try HS400 and fall back to HS200. The first MEK build's fallback shared the new
  clock and the tuning: at 166 MHz the tuned tap 58 failed CMD6/CMD8 on most boots, both
  attempts failed, and pb came up without its eMMC (no `part write`). The third reset got
  through and BOOT1 was restored from the test bench's `pb_signed.imx`. Since then the MEK's
  fallback is the old path exactly: PLL1 at 200 MHz, tap 18, no tuning (`fixed_tap`).
- The model: MIX_CTRL's HS400 bits, the strobe DLL's lock, HS400 data that samples only with
  both; the i.MX6UL has neither. Tests: the 8MP's HS400 ES and HS200 configurations, a strobe
  DLL that doesn't lock then the HS200 init, ES on a card without it, HS400 after tuning,
  200 MHz in (HS400 at 100 MHz), `fixed_tap`. A mutant without the strobe DLL fails four.
  `scripts/pbdev test` 125 of 125; jiffy, imx8dxlevk, imx8mevk, pico8ml, the 8MP EVK and the
  MEK build without warnings.
- On the bench (`boot-time-image`, medians of 5, System B overwritten):

  | Board      | Mode                  | Read MiB/s | 10 MiB load + verify | 29 MiB load + verify |
  | ---------- | --------------------- | ---------- | -------------------- | -------------------- |
  | 8MP EVK    | HS200 (same tree)     | 88         | 129 ms               | 346 ms               |
  | 8MP EVK    | HS400 ES, 200 MHz     | 162        | 95 ms                | 252 ms               |
  | MEK        | HS200 (test bench pb) | 173        | 68 ms                | 181 ms               |
  | MEK        | HS400, 198 MHz        | 255        | 53 ms                | 128 ms               |

  Both: 100 MiB random written to Root B and `part verify` ok, resets (8MP five, MEK ten),
  Linux to the login and back after `reboot`. The 8MP's eMMC init is ~1.4 ms shorter (no
  tuning); its hash (122 MiB/s, CAAM) is now slower than the read. The MEK's tuning alternates
  between two windows (F-471), both taps checked.
- Boards now: the 8MP EVK BOOT1 (enabled) and the MEK BOOT1 (enabled) run this tree
  (`593201fd`'s); BOOT0 unchanged on both.
- Committed: `67cba6b3` (the driver, the model, the tests), `fb6a6ded` (the 8MP EVK),
